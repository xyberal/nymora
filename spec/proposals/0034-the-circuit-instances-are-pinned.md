# Proposal 0034 — The circuit's concrete instances are pinned

**Status:** **Applied** — the sections below are now normative in the specification
**Affects:** §5.2, §6.5, §9.1
**Builds on:** [proposal 0033](0033-the-proving-system-is-plonkish-kzg-over-bls12-381.md)
(the proving system is Plonkish KZG over BLS12-381, which left the instance-level
facts as its two open questions) and [proposal 0032](0032-the-depth-constant-is-thirty-two.md)
(`PROTOCOL_DEPTH = 32`, the size every figure below is measured at)

---

## Problem

Proposal 0033 named the family — Plonkish arithmetization, KZG commitments,
BLS12-381, Poseidon as the circuit hash, EdDSA over Jubjub with a Poseidon
transcript as the certificate scheme — and deliberately left three things at the
instance level unpinned: the Poseidon parameter provenance (its open question 1),
the reference-string excerpt and its chain of custody (its open question 2), and
the certificate scheme's exact equation, which "EdDSA over Jubjub with a Poseidon
transcript" names as a construction but not as bytes.

The real circuit cannot be written past these gaps. Conformance vectors cannot
regenerate until the hash's round constants are fixed to the digit; the certificate
gadget cannot be reviewed until its verification equation is stated; and a proving
key is only as trustworthy as the provenance of the string it was derived from.
Each pin is also the kind of decision that goes wrong when made on paper, so every
figure in this proposal was taken from a running circuit first: the full expensive
core of §9.1's chain — the five-input leaf commitment, three depth-32 Poseidon
Merkle paths, the epoch-key correspondence, the in-circuit certificate
verification, and two nullifier derivations — was implemented, proven, and
verified before any of the numbers below were written down.

## Decision

### 1. The Poseidon instance

**Width 3 (rate 2, capacity 1), α = 5, 8 full and 60 partial rounds, over the
BLS12-381 scalar field**, with the MDS matrix and round constants generated by the
Grain-LFSR procedure of the Poseidon reference implementation, reproducible as:

```
sage generate_parameters_grain.sage 1 0 255 3 8 60 \
  0x73eda753299d7d483339d80809a1d80553bda402fffe5bfeffffffff00000001
```

(the script from the `daira/pasta-hadeshash` repository; the final argument is the
BLS12-381 scalar-field modulus). The specification pins the procedure and this
invocation; the generated constants are additionally committed with a checksum so
that a reviewer can verify either from the other.

Two honesty notes. The reference round-count calculation yields 57 partial rounds
for this shape at the 128-bit level; the pinned instance carries three more —
strictly added margin, and, decisively, it is the instance a maintained
implementation ships and runs in production rather than a bespoke 57-round instance
nobody does. And the `measure/` harness's figures (proposals 0031, 0032) were taken
on a 57-partial-round instance: the three extra rounds move the per-level Merkle
cost by about one percent and move no decision threshold; the harness README gains
a note rather than a re-run.

This is the instance behind every `Hash(...)` a statement computes: the §5.2
accumulators, the leaf commitment, the nullifiers and pseudonyms, the certificate
payloads, and the exclusion accumulators' keyed structure.

### 2. The certificate scheme, as an equation

**EdDSA over Jubjub in (R, S) form with a Poseidon transcript.** Keys are scalars
of Jubjub's prime-order subgroup, `PK = sk·G`. Signing a canonical payload
compressed to a field element `m`:

- the nonce `k` is derived deterministically from `(sk, m)` — an RFC-8032-style
  obligation on the signer, costing the circuit nothing and required so that
  vectors are byte-reproducible and no signer ever risks nonce reuse;
- `R = k·G`;
- `e = Poseidon(R.x, R.y, PK.x, PK.y, m)`, interpreted little-endian and reduced
  into the Jubjub scalar field;
- `S = k + e·sk`.

The signature is `(R, S)`: 32 bytes of compressed point and 32 bytes of scalar —
64 bytes, the width (and the 32-byte public key width) of the provisional scheme it
replaces, so no wire format above the primitive moves. Verification, in-circuit and
out, recomputes `e` and checks `S·G = R + e·PK`.

Jubjub has cofactor 8; the equation is stated over the prime-order subgroup, and
**subgroup membership of `R` and `PK` is part of the statement** — a witness point
off the subgroup must fail to prove, and a negative vector pins that (open
question 3).

This resolves 0033's construction into bytes. The measured cost stands: verifying
this equation in-circuit prices the same as the challenge-form variant it was
checked against, and the running core carries it.

### 3. The reference string

**Inherited from Filecoin's phase-1 powers of tau over BLS12-381**, canonical
source `trusted-setup.filecoin.io` (`phase1radix2m19`, 2^19 powers) — one of the
two ceremony lineages 0033 named, with the larger public record of independent
contributors. The running core needs 2^13 rows; the inherited string covers 2^19,
a 64-fold margin over today's figure.

Chain of custody: the repository commits the excerpt sized to the final circuit,
the provenance chain from the ceremony transcript to the excerpt (every
transformation named and reproducible), and SHA-256 checksums of both. The
verification key is deterministic public preprocessing over that excerpt, exactly
as 0033's setup-model argument requires.

Tests and CI use a locally generated, deterministically seeded, **insecure** string
(`unsafe_setup`) so the gate runs offline and byte-stable; it is never serialized
into the repository and never used to derive an artifact that ships. Production
proving and verification keys derive from the Filecoin string only.

## The running core, as evidence

The chain statement's expensive core — leaf commitment
`Poseidon(pk_root.x, pk_root.y, sk_cred, r_root, agora)`, three depth-32 Poseidon
Merkle paths, the epoch-key correspondence `pk_epoch = sk_epoch·G`, the full
in-circuit certificate verification above, and two Poseidon nullifier derivations —
proven and verified end to end on the pinned instances (2026-08-16, laptop-class
Apple-silicon hardware, insecure local string):

- **rows:** 2^13 (`optimal_k = 13`)
- **prove:** ~350 ms — §6.5's phone-prover requirement holds with two orders of
  magnitude of hardware between this laptop and its floor
- **verify:** ~1.9 ms — §7's per-read verification is a non-cost
- **proof:** 3,296 bytes, fixed size

One figure moved against expectation and is recorded rather than rounded away:
0033 argued proof size around "~1 KB" as inside §6.5's "small, fixed-size blob."
The measured blob is 3.3 KB — fixed, uniform across every action, and still orders
of magnitude below the content it travels with, but three times the working
estimate. §6.5's requirement is shape and smallness, not a kilobyte; the number is
pinned here so nobody inherits the estimate as a fact.

## Alternatives rejected

- **The dusk-network stack** (`dusk-plonk` + `dusk-poseidon` + `jubjub-schnorr`).
  The strongest alternative: pure-Rust PLONK/KZG on BLS12-381, and its core is
  audited. Rejected on instance mismatch and assembly risk: its Poseidon is a
  width-5 Hades instance — a different shape from the rate-2 instance every
  measurement and accumulator in this protocol is built on — so the statement would
  be hand-assembled at the composer-gate level, reintroducing exactly the
  hand-built certificate-gadget risk 0033 flagged as highest; and its reference
  string flows through its own ceremony tooling rather than a documented import of
  the lineages 0033 named.
- **The Axiom halo2 lineage** (`halo2-axiom`, `halo2-lib`). The deepest tooling,
  centered on BN254 — the curve 0033 rejected on lifetime margin. Building it over
  BLS12-381 means maintaining the port alone.
- **Raw gadget construction over a bare proof system.** Composing the statement
  from maintained, production-exercised chips (ECC, Poseidon, decomposition) is the
  entire risk argument for the posture below; bypassing them buys nothing and
  reopens the gadget-soundness class of failure — the halo2_gadgets ecc-mul flaw
  (disclosed 2026-05, four years in production) is the standing example.

## Implementation posture (informative, 2026-08)

The running core is built on **midnight-zk** (`midnight-zk-stdlib` 2.3.5,
`midnight-circuits` 7.2.4, `midnight-proofs` 0.8.2, `midnight-curves` 0.3.1):
halo2-v0.3.0 lineage rebuilt on BLS12-381 with native Jubjub, Apache-2.0/MIT
licensing, releases current to this month, and a first-party loader for the
Filecoin string. The pinned Poseidon instance and the certificate equation are the
ones its maintained chips compute, which is not a coincidence: pinning the instance
a production implementation ships is the low-risk direction, provided the
provenance is independently reproducible — and it is (decision 1).

Posture, honestly: the proofs layer descends from widely-reviewed halo2; the
circuits layer states plainly that it has not been audited. The certificate gadget
is therefore no longer the *hand-written* highest-risk component 0033 named — it
composes maintained chips — but the risk is downgraded, not retired. The
mitigations already mandated stand and now have addresses: negative vectors per
statement clause, subgroup-membership vectors for every witness point, and the
blinding-sufficiency review §15 names before any privacy claim rests on the
implementation. This section is dated observation, not a binding; the pins above
outlive any library.

## Open questions

1. **Canonical payload encodings.** How each certificate payload and each
   `Hash(...)` argument list maps to field elements (ordering, domain separation,
   length framing) must be pinned clause by clause when the vectors regenerate —
   the same discipline the byte-level encodings went through, one level down.
2. **The exclusion accumulators' concrete structure.** §9.1 fixes them as keyed
   Poseidon structures; whether the absence clause is proven against a sorted
   Merkle structure or an indexed map, and what it costs in rows, is decided with
   the full statement's row budget when the real circuit lands.
3. **Subgroup enforcement, verified.** Confirm the point-assignment path constrains
   witness points into the prime-order subgroup, and pin it with a negative vector
   (a cofactor-torsion point must fail to prove).
4. **The constants artifact.** Commit the generated Poseidon constants with their
   checksum and a regeneration note, so reviewers can verify the code against the
   pinned invocation without trusting either.

## Consequences

- The real circuit proceeds against these pins at `PROTOCOL_DEPTH = 32`, behind the
  existing `ProofSystem` boundary, replacing the stub.
- When applied, §5.2 and §6.5 name the Poseidon instance at parameter level, §9.1
  states the certificate equation and widths, and §6.5's reference-string sentence
  gains the named source and custody rule. 0033's two open questions close.
- The conformance vectors regenerate only with the real-circuit swap — one
  synchronized change across specification, code, and vectors — carrying the
  encoding pins of open question 1 with it.
- The provisional hash and signature retire with that same swap, as 0033 already
  records; nothing retires at proposal time.
